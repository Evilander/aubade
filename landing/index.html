A morning radio station that knows you

An aubade is the oldest kind of poem — a song for the dawn, usually sung by someone reluctant to leave the night behind. This one is smaller and more practical. Every morning it spends three minutes telling the story of a yesterday and the shape of a today: what got committed while the world slept, which projects have gone quiet, the single thing worth finishing before the coffee wears off. It reads like someone who has been watching, kindly, for a long while.

A sample broadcast

Read this in a voice that isn't trying to sell you something:

“It's Tuesday, April sixteenth, and the sun came up at six fifty-three. You pushed four commits to Audrey last night — the memory reconsolidation pass is in. Three projects are cold, but genesis-prism blinked last Thursday, so it's not dead. One thing to do today, and only one: finish the landing page. Ninety minutes. Coffee. Go.”
